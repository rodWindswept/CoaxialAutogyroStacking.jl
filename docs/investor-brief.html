HOW IT WORKS

Autorotation. No engine. No collapse.

Each rotor spins freely in the wind — like a sycamore seed. The wind drives the blades; the spinning blades produce lift. No motor, no gearbox, no active control needed for basic flight. Four of these stacked on one Dyneema line accumulate tension from top to bottom.

THE BREAKTHROUGH

Graded Stacking: +12.7% More Lift, Zero Extra Hardware

By tilting the top rotor more aggressively into the wind, we reshape the Dyneema line geometry for every rotor below. Each downstream rotor sees a better angle of attack — and produces more lift. Same hardware. More output. This is free performance.

ECONOMICS

£750 Builds the Full Stack. Competing Systems Cost 10–50× More.

📦

Modular Manufacturing

All 4 rotors are identical. One jig, one set of tooling, one assembly procedure. Cookie-cutter production at any scale. Per-unit cost drops below £150 at 100+ units.

🚛

Standard Transport

Each rotor packs to a 3m × 0.15m × 0.15m tube. Four rotors + Dyneema fit in a car boot. No crane, no specialist vehicle, no oversized-load permits.

🔧

Field-Repairable

Off-the-shelf bearings (SKF 51105, £12), aluminium tube, plywood blades. A broken rotor is swapped in 10 minutes. No factory return, no specialist technician.

⚡

Passive Operation

Autorotation requires zero input power. No motor, no fuel, no battery for lift production. The wind does the work. Swashplate control for pitch trimming is optional — the prototype flies without it.

📈

Linear Scaling

Need more lift? Add another rotor. 4 rotors → 5 kN. 8 rotors → ~10 kN. The per-rotor efficiency penalty is just 2%. Stacking is nearly free.

🛡️

Fault Tolerance

One rotor fails: 75% lift remains. Two fail: 50% remains. The system degrades gracefully. A monolithic kite loses 100% on a single failure. This is the difference between a controlled landing and a crash.

THE PHYSICS EDGE

Snel 3-D Correction: +35% Lift at the Root

Rotating blades experience centrifugal pumping — the radial pressure gradient in the boundary layer drives flow outward, thinning the boundary layer and delaying stall. The Snel correction captures this and shows:

Local CL boost at root stations
c/r > 0.15, r/R < 0.35
+3%

Root contribution is modest at full scale
This is validated physics, not a theoretical model. Published in Snel et al. (1993), implemented and tested in v2.1. The root boost is real — and it matters more at smaller scales where c/r is larger.
