# SPEC.md — bem-tension-accum

## Slug
`bem-tension-accum`

## Title
Tension Accumulation Along the Polygon Line — R=3.0m, N=4, graded, 45°

## Data Source
Generated by `compute_tension_profile.jl` using the polygon line solver
and BEM v2.0 autorotation model. Uses `stack_tension_profile()` which
calls the full polygon chain solver for each wind speed. Output: CSV.

## Variables

- **X-axis:** Distance from anchor (m). 0 = anchor (ground), 60m = topmost rotor.
- **Y-axis:** Cumulative line tension (N). 0 at top, increasing downward.
- **Color:** Wind speed (4–16 m/s), viridis gradient.
- **Vertical markers:** Rotor positions (R1 nearest anchor, R4 at top).

## Plain-language explanation

This chart shows how mechanical tension builds up along the line connecting
stacked autogyro rotors. The anchor (at 0m, bottom) must hold the weight
and drag of everything above it. The topmost rotor (at 60m) only carries
its own load — nothing pulls from above, so tension there is near zero.

Each colored line represents a different wind speed. At 4 m/s the rotors
barely pull; at 16 m/s the tension is enormous (~20 kN). The spacing
between lines grows with wind speed because aerodynamic forces scale
roughly with v².

The dotted vertical lines show where each rotor sits. Between rotors,
tension increases from bare-line drag and the line's own weight — the
polygon solver accounts for these effects.

## Message

Anchor tension is the critical structural constraint. At 16 m/s, 4 × 3m
rotors in graded tilt at 45° elevation produce over 20 kN — equivalent
to supporting 2 tonnes. The anchor line and ground attachment must be
rated accordingly.

## Audience

- **Structural engineers** sizing anchor lines and ground anchors.
- **Safety reviewers** assessing worst-case loads.
- **Designers** choosing between more rotors (more lift) and more tension.
